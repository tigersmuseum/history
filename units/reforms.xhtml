In 1661, Charles II issued a Royal Warrant that created the first regiments of what would become the British Army.

The Militia Act of 1661 declared that the monarch was in supreme command of the army and navy for the defence of the realm.

The Militia Act of 1662 codified the powers of lord-lieutenants in England and Wales to raise the militia.

The Recruiting Act of 1703 provided for the forcible enlistment of able bodied men into the army and navy who did not have visible means of subsistence.

In 1751, a Royal Warrant decreed that all regiments would be known by their number and not by the Colonel’s name.

The Militia Act of 1757 led to the rapid expansion of the British Militia.

The Militia Act of 1762 consolidated the acts related to the Militia of Great Britain.

On 21 August 1782, the Commander-in-Chief of the Forces issued a regulation giving an English county designation to each regiment of foot other than those with a royal title or highland regiments.

The Volunteer Act of 1794 established a voluntary military reserve force.

The Provisional Cavalry Act of 1796 established the Provisional Cavalry.

The Militia Act of 1852 consolidated and amended enactments related to the militia of the United Kingdom.

The Ordnance Board Transfer Act of 1855 dissolved the Ordnance Board and transferred its responsibilities to the War Department.

Volunteer Force: On 12th May 1859, the Secretary of State for War authorised the formation of volunteer rifle corps, and of artillery corps in defended coastal towns.

The Regulation of the Forces Act of 1871 (Cardwell) organized line infantry regiments and recruitment along local lines.

The Imperial Yeomanry was established on 2 January 1900 by Royal Warrant issued on 24 December 1899.

The Childers reforms of 1881 established by General Order 41/1881, issued on 1 May 1881, and amended by G.O. 70/1881 dated 1 July.

The Territorial and Reserve Forces Act of 1907 transferred existing Volunteer and Yeomanry units into a new Territorial Force (TF), and disbanded the Militia to form a new Special Reserve of the Regular Army.

The Territorial Force replaced the Volunteer Force in 1908.

The Special Reserve was established on 1 April 1908.

Lord Kitchener was appointed Secretary of State for War on 6 August 1914, and immediately began to organise the New Army.

The Military Service Act of 1916 imposed conscription in Great Britain, but not in Ireland or any other British jurisdiction.

The Territorial Army and Militia Act of 1921 renamed the Territorial Force as the "Territorial Army" and the Special Reserve as the "Militia".

The Reserve Forces Act of 1937 allowed "Class A" British Army reservists to be called up for active service during their first five years after leaving the Army.

The National Service (Armed Forces) Act of 1939 enforced full conscription on all male British subjects between 18 and 41 who were present in Great Britain, lasting until the end of "the present emergency".

The National Service Act of 1948 extended the British conscription of the Second World War in the form of "National Service".

The Army Reserve Act of 1950 consolidated enactments relating to the army reserve.

In 1990, the Secretary of State for Defence announced Options for Change; a restructuring of the British Armed Forces following the end of the Cold War.
