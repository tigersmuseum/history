In 1661, Charles II issued a Royal Warrant that created the first regiments of what would become the British Army.

The Recruiting Act of 1703 provided for the forcible enlistment of able bodied men into the army and navy who did not have visible means of subsistence.

In 1751, a Royal Warrant decreed that all regiments would be known by their number and not by the Colonel’s name.

The Militia Act of 1757 led to the rapid expansion of the British Militia.

The Militia Act of 1762 consolidated the acts related to the Militia of Great Britain.

The Volunteer Act of 1794 established a voluntary military reserve force.

The Provisional Cavalry Act of 1796 established the Provisional Cavalry.

The Ordnance Board Transfer Act of 1855 dissolved the Ordnance Board and transferred its responsibilities to the War Department.

Volunteer Force: On 12th May 1859, the Secreterary of State for War authorised the formation of volunteer rifle corps, and of artillery corps in defended coastal towns.

The Regulation of the Forces Act of 1871 (Cardwell) organized line infantry regiments and recruitment along local lines.

The Imperial Yeomanry was established on 2 January 1900 by Royal Warrant issued on 24 December 1899.

The Childers reforms of 1881 established by General Order 41/1881, issued on 1 May 1881, and amended by G.O. 70/1881 dated 1 July.

The Territorial and Reserve Forces Act of 1907 reformed the auxiliary forces of the British Army by transferring existing Volunteer and Yeomanry units into a new Territorial Force (TF); and disbanding the Militia to form a new Special Reserve of the Regular Army.

The Territorial Force replaced the Volunteer Force in 1908.

The Special Reserve was established on 1st April 1908.
